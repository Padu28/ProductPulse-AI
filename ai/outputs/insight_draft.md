Observations
The largest user-funnel drop-off is at View item to Add to cart. “User funnel: largest step drop-off” is 79.52% (48,707 of 61,252 users with ‘View item’ did not reach ‘Add to cart’; segment: View item to Add to cart, 2020-11-01 to 2021-01-31). The matching step conversion, “User funnel: View item to Add to cart”, is 20.48% (12,545 / 61,252; All users). This is stage presence, not sequence. [funnel_user_largest_drop_off, funnel_user_view_to_cart]
User purchase conversion is similar across first observed device, all within 0.08 percentage points over the same period. “User purchase conversion: desktop” is 1.60% (2,518 / 156,905 users), “mobile” is 1.68% (1,802 / 107,175 users) and “tablet” is 1.63% (99 / 6,074 users). The all-user “User purchase conversion” is 1.64% (4,419 / 270,154 active users). The tablet figure rests on only 99 purchasers. [device_desktop_purchase_conversion, device_mobile_purchase_conversion, device_tablet_purchase_conversion, user_purchase_conversion]
Most users are observed on a single day, and observed return activity is low. “Users active on one day only” is 89.00% (240,441 / 270,154 users; All users). “Activity retention: 2020-11 cohort, month 1” is 5.86% (4,651 / 79,421 users in cohort) and “month 2” is 1.52% (1,204 / 79,421). “Activity retention: 2020-12 cohort, month 1” is 2.53% (2,517 / 99,664 users in cohort). [single_day_users, retention_2020-11_month_1, retention_2020-11_month_2, retention_2020-12_month_1]
Hypotheses (unproven)
Hypothesis (unproven): The gap between the user-level and in-session rates for Add to cart to Begin checkout may reflect users starting checkout in a later session than the one in which they added to cart. The user-level rate is 77.44% (9,715 / 12,545 users) and the sequential session rate is 35.71% (5,416 / 15,167 sessions). The two metrics differ in unit and in ordering rules, so the gap could also be definitional. [funnel_user_cart_to_checkout, funnel_session_cart_to_checkout]
Hypothesis (unproven): Users first acquired through Referral may arrive with higher purchase intent than those from other named channels. “User purchase conversion: Referral” is 1.86% (744 / 40,019 users), against 1.58% for Direct (1,011 / 64,109), 1.53% for Organic search (1,557 / 101,742) and 1.53% for Paid search (218 / 14,282). The supplied data does not show whether this difference is statistically meaningful or what drives it. [channel_referral_purchase_conversion, channel_direct_purchase_conversion, channel_organic_search_purchase_conversion, channel_paid_search_purchase_conversion]
Recommended next analyses
Build a sequential, user-level funnel across sessions (View item, Add to cart, Begin checkout, Purchase, in order). This would show how much of the user-versus-session gap is cross-session completion and how much comes from the any-order definition.
Break the View item to Add to cart step down by product and category. Among the top five items by viewers, viewer-to-buyer rates range from 1.57% (Google Zip Hoodie F/C, 218 / 13,869) to 0.36% (Google Campus Bike Eco Tee Navy, 41 / 11,339). Item-level add-to-cart rates are not supplied and would be needed. [product_top2_viewer_to_buyer, product_top5_viewer_to_buyer]
Add confidence intervals to the device and channel conversion comparisons, and re-cut retention by weekly cohorts with equal observation windows. This would show which segment differences exceed noise and remove the bias from incomplete late cohorts.
Limitations
Sample: This is a historical, obfuscated GA4 sample (Google Merchandise Store) covering only 2020-11-01 to 2021-01-31. Revenue ($308,200.00 across 4,451 validated orders) is not the store’s real revenue. [revenue_usd, validated_orders]
Causality: The data is observational, so no metric here establishes a cause.
Funnel definitions: User-level funnels are stage presence in any order over the whole period. Session funnels are sequential but miss purchases that complete a cart from an earlier session. The two are not directly comparable.
Users: Counts are pseudonymous browser/device ids, not people. Device is the first observed device per user.
Channels: Attribution is first-touch and user-level. “Other” (1.44%, 680 / 47,101) and “Data deleted” (7.20%, 209 / 2,901) are obfuscation artefacts and should not be read as real channels. [channel_other_purchase_conversion, channel_data_deleted_purchase_conversion]
Retention: Cohorts are based on first observed activity, not true first visit. With only three months of data, late cohorts are incomplete and late arrivals have had less time to return, which inflates the single-day share.
Missing evidence: No prior-period comparison, significance tests, or item-level add-to-cart metrics are supplied, so trends and the reliability of small segment differences (notably tablet) cannot be assessed.
